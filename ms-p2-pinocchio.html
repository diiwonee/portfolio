01

Project Overview

The Pinocchio problem is a profit maximisation linear program with integer decision variables — meaning we need whole numbers of trucks and trains, not fractions. It introduces a demand cap constraint (at most 2,000 trucks/month) and two resource constraints (carpentry and finishing labour).

The objective: maximise monthly profit = total revenue − material cost − labour cost, subject to labour availability and demand limits.

LP Formulation
max Z = 450x₁ + 550x₂
Subject to
Carpentry: x₁ + 2x₂ ≤ 5,000
Finishing: x₁ + x₂ ≤ 3,000
Demand: x₁ ≤ 2,000
Non-negativity: x₁, x₂ ≥ 0; x₁, x₂ ∈ ℤ

03

Evidence & Solution

Solution
The classic solution: produce 1,000 trucks and 2,000 trains = 1,550,000 CZK monthly profit. The binding constraints are carpentry (1000+4000=5000) and finishing (1000+2000=3000). The demand cap is slack.
Integer DVs
Pinocchio must produce whole toys — you can't make 1.7 trains. This makes the problem an Integer Linear Program (ILP). In practice, for large-scale problems, ILP is far harder to solve computationally than continuous LP.
Activity
Completed in class via spreadsheet (Google Sheets), with corner point enumeration. Later, class worked on building a general LP solver using AI — generating code, testing it, and hosting on GitHub Pages.
04

What Did I Learn?

Knowledge
Integer LP vs continuous LP. Why integer constraints dramatically increase computational complexity. How to identify binding vs slack constraints at the optimal solution.
Skills
Calculating unit profits from component costs. Setting up LP in spreadsheet. Corner point enumeration. Understanding why the optimal is always a vertex.
Reflection
The most satisfying moment was checking the solution: both constraints exactly met, no slack anywhere. That precision — the math using every available resource perfectly — felt almost aesthetically pleasing. It's why people enjoy optimisation.
