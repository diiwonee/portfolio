Project Overview

This project solves a linear programming problem from class: a street food company wants to invest a budget of 360,000 in two types of food trucks — taco trucks and burrito trucks — to maximise the total number of weekly customers.

The challenge: each truck type has a different cost, a maximum availability limit, and a different customer draw per week. We need to find the exact combination that gets the most customers without exceeding the budget or availability caps.

LP Formulation
Maximise Z = 2,000x₁ + 5,000x₂   (total weekly customers)
Subject to
Budget:   30,000x₁ + 40,000x₂ ≤ 360,000   → simplified: 3x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 36
Taco availability:   x₁ ≤ 8
Burrito availability:   x₂ ≤ 6
Non-negativity:   x₁, x₂ ≥ 0

Interactive Demonstration

Feasible region — x₁ (taco trucks) vs x₂ (burrito trucks). Shaded area = all valid solutions. ★ = optimal point.
Reading the Graph

Every point inside the shaded region is a valid solution

The three constraint lines form the boundary of the feasible region. The budget line (3x₁ + 4x₂ = 36) is the tightest constraint — it cuts diagonally. The optimal solution is always at one of the corner points where constraint lines intersect. The star ★ marks the optimal: x₁ = 4, x₂ = 6.

The optimal solution to any LP problem always lies at a corner point (vertex) of the feasible region. We evaluate the objective function Z = 2,000x₁ + 5,000x₂ at each corner and pick the highest.

Optimal Solution

Buy 4 taco trucks + 6 burrito trucks = 46,000 customers/week

Total cost: 4 × 30,000 + 6 × 40,000 = 120,000 + 240,000 = 360,000 — exactly at budget. The burrito truck availability constraint (x₂ ≤ 6) is binding. We use all 6 available burrito trucks because they attract 2.5× more customers per truck than taco trucks.

Slope of the objective function: Rearranging Z = 2,000x₁ + 5,000x₂ for x₂ gives: x₂ = Z/5,000 − (2/5)x₁. The slope is −2/5 = −0.4. Since this is less steep than the budget constraint slope (−3/4 = −0.75), the OF "slides" toward the x₂ = 6 corner before hitting the budget line — confirming the optimal is at (4, 6).

Evidence from Coursework

This problem was given as a class activity titled "Test Your Ability" — a self-assessed LP problem to check understanding of graphical solution methods after the Pizza Optimizer and Pinocchio problems.

Step 1
Identified the objective function: Z = 2,000x₁ + 5,000x₂. Goal is maximisation of weekly customers.
Step 2
Identified all constraints: budget (30,000x₁ + 40,000x₂ ≤ 360,000), availability (x₁ ≤ 8, x₂ ≤ 6), and non-negativity.
Step 3
Graphed the feasible region by plotting each constraint line and shading the valid area. Found 4 corner points to evaluate.

Calculated Z at each corner. Maximum Z = 46,000 customers/week at (x₁ = 4, x₂ = 6). Confirmed by slope analysis of the objective function.

04

What Did I Learn?

Knowledge
How to formulate a real LP problem from a word problem. Why the optimal solution is always at a corner point of the feasible region. How the slope of the objective function determines which corner is optimal.
Skills
Translating business constraints into mathematical inequalities. Graphing feasible regions. Evaluating corner points systematically. Verifying algebraically with slope analysis.
Reflection
What surprised me: even though taco trucks are cheaper, the optimal solution uses fewer of them. The burrito truck is 2.5× more valuable per customer attracted — so the model naturally prioritises maxing out burrito trucks first, then filling the remaining budget. Intuition says "buy cheap" — the model says "buy efficient." The model wins.
