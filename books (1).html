Diana's Picks

One about the cost of ambition. One about the cost of vanity. Both about what we trade away to become who we think we want to be.

ME
Martin Eden
Jack London · 1909
Ambition · Tragedy · Class

A working-class sailor teaches himself to become a writer through sheer, obsessive will. He reads every book he can find, writes until his hands ache, and slowly claws his way toward the literary elite — only to discover that success delivers nothing he actually wanted.

"He was mastered by the verb 'to be,' and he worshipped it as the greatest of all verbs."
Rate this book
★ ★ ★ ★ ★
Be the first to rate this
Okay so this book will make you feel simultaneously inspired and deeply depressed. Martin grinds harder than anyone you've ever met, achieves everything he wanted, and then is just... empty. It's a masterclass in "be careful what you wish for." Also Jack London clearly had some unresolved issues with critics. 10/10 recommend if you enjoy suffering with your morning coffee.
DG
The Picture of Dorian Gray
Oscar Wilde · 1891
Vanity · Corruption · Beauty

A beautiful young man makes a Faustian bargain: his portrait ages while he stays forever young and perfect. He then spends decades doing increasingly horrible things, safe in the knowledge that his face shows nothing. Until it does.

"The only way to get rid of a temptation is to yield to it."
Rate this book
★ ★ ★ ★ ★
Be the first to rate this
Lord Henry is the most charismatic bad influence in all of literature and honestly I understand why Dorian fell for it. This book is essentially "what if the most charming man alive just... talked you into ruining your life?" Wilde writes like every sentence costs him something beautiful to say. The ending is perfect and horrible. Read it.
