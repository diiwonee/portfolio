01

Project Overview

This project explores autarky — a state of complete economic self-sufficiency where a nation refuses all trade with the outside world. The assignment asked for a fiction story integrating course concepts: autarky, protectionism, tariffs, the gravity model, and globalization.

Inspired by Yevgeny Zamyatin's novel We (1924) — a foundational dystopian text about a perfectly rational, isolated civilization — the story below follows a similar world six chapters at a time, showing how absolute autarky produces both stability and stagnation, both safety and suffocation.

Autarky

Self-sufficiency as ideology

A state produces everything it needs internally. No imports, no exports, no external dependencies. Theoretically safe from global shocks — but also from growth.

The Alternative

Comparative advantage

Trade allows specialisation: make what you're relatively better at, exchange for the rest. Even weak economies gain from trade. Autarky gives up this gain entirely.

03

Evidence & Concepts

The assignment required integrating course topics into a cohesive fiction. Below is the concept map linking story events to International Trade theory.

Autarky
The Collective lives in complete self-sufficiency. No trade routes, no foreign goods. The Wall is both physical and ideological — the perfect autarkic barrier.
Gravity Model
When Mira discovers maps of distant lands, she realizes trade volume once correlated with distance and size — the Collective traded with its closest neighbors most. The model predicted it; the Collapse severed it.
Comparative Advantage
The Collective can grow grain efficiently but cannot produce medicine. Outside traders have medicine but poor grain yields. Ricardo would say: trade is beneficial for both. The Elders refuse.
Globalization
Chapter VI's ending — the Wall fractures — represents the irreversibility of contact. Once seen, the outside world cannot be unseen. Globalization, like knowledge, cannot be un-spread.
04

What Did I Learn?

Knowledge
Autarky is not simply "no trade" — it is an active, sustained ideological commitment that requires constant suppression of information about the outside world to maintain. It cannot survive contact.
Skills
Synthesising economic theory into creative narrative. Understanding how concepts like comparative advantage aren't just mathematical — they have human stakes. Writing fiction as a tool for economic reasoning.
Reflection
The most difficult part was making autarky feel internally logical — because from inside it, it makes sense. The Collective isn't evil; it's afraid. And fear, it turns out, is the most common real-world reason for protectionism.
