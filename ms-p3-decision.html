01

Project Overview

This project used the Decision Tree Scenario Activity from class. Groups of three built a tree based on one of three scenarios, reaching a minimum depth of Level 5, with at least two branches at every non-terminal node.

I chose Scenario 3: The Viral Product Launch. A wearable technology device suddenly goes globally viral after a celebrity mention. The company must decide: expand rapidly, hold steady, or pivot strategy?

Scenario 3 — Selected

The Viral Product Launch

Your company develops a wearable technology device that suddenly becomes globally popular after a celebrity mentions it online. Orders explode overnight. Investors demand rapid expansion, customers expect immediate shipping, and competitors are already reacting. Expanding too quickly could damage product quality, but moving too slowly could allow competitors to take the market.

Construction Rules
1Root = Level 0. Minimum depth: Level 5
2At least one path must reach Level 5
3Every non-terminal node → at least 2 branches
4Include both positive and negative outcomes
5Consequences must be realistic and logically connected
6Labels, arrows, and short descriptions required

03

Evidence from Coursework

Activity
Group activity (groups of 3). Each group chose one scenario, then each member built 4–6 levels of branches for their assigned decision branch. The group combined the three sub-trees into one complete decision tree.
My branch
I developed the "Expand Rapidly" branch. My path traces: rapid expansion → quality issues → recall → restructure → either recover or collapse. Five levels, both positive and negative terminal nodes.
Key insight
A single early choice (expand vs hold) creates fundamentally different outcome spaces. By Level 5, the two paths have almost no overlap. This is why decision trees are valuable: they make explicit how much early choices constrain later options.
04

What Did I Learn?

Knowledge
Decision tree structure: nodes, branches, levels, terminal nodes. How probability and consequence combine in expected value calculations. The difference between risk (known probabilities) and uncertainty (unknown probabilities).
Skills
Systematic consequence mapping. Thinking five steps ahead from a single decision. Identifying which branches are realistic vs which are wishful thinking.
Reflection
The hardest part was making the branches realistic — not just "things go well" vs "things go badly," but specific, connected consequences. A recall doesn't just hurt sales — it destroys trust, triggers investor panic, and may force structural changes. Depth of thinking, not just branching, is the skill.
